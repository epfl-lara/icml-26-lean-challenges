- Complete source proof text:
  > Since `⟨LT(f_1), ..., LT(f_s)⟩ ⊊ ⟨LT(I)⟩`, there exists an element `f ∈ I` such that `LT(f) ∉ ⟨LT(f_1), ..., LT(f_s)⟩`. Divide `f` by `B = {f_1, ..., f_s}`. By the division algorithm, we get `f = ∑ q_i f_i + r`, where `r` is the remainder, and no term of `r` is divisible by any `LT(f_i)`. Suppose, for contradiction, that `r = 0`. Then `f = ∑ q_i f_i`. By the division algorithm, every nonzero term `q_i f_i` has leading term no larger than `LT(f)`. Hence the leading term of `f` must be divisible by one of the `LT(f_i)`. Therefore `LT(f) ∈ ⟨LT(f_1), ..., LT(f_s)⟩`, contradicting the choice of `f`. Thus `r ≠ 0`. Hence there exists `f ∈ I` whose remainder on division by `B` is nonzero.
- Source proof / prover notes:
  1. From strict containment, show not every leading term of an element of `I` lies in `leadingTermIdeal m (Set.range f)`; choose `p ∈ I` with `m.leadingTerm p` outside that ideal.
  2. Use `hf_ne` and field coefficients to obtain unit leading coefficients, then apply `MonomialOrder.div` to divide `p` by `f` and get `q` and `r` satisfying `IsDivisionRemainder`.
  3. Assume `r = 0`. From the division decomposition and degree bounds, show the leading term of `p` is in the monomial ideal generated by the leading terms of the `f i`, contradicting the choice of `p`.
  4. Conclude `r ≠ 0` and package `p`, `q`, and `r`.
